Details

—

Routes and contract addresses mirror https://bridge.zaphod.one/api/state (fetched 2026-11-07). Sends from 42 Chain / BSC / Ethereum go through the Zaphod BridgeRouter: approve the token to the BridgeRouter, then transferRemote with the messaging fee from a fresh quoteNative call paid on top. Sends from QL1 have no BridgeRouter: the token is approved to the Hyperlane warp router and transferRemote is called with value 0. Arrival is checked on the destination chain's Hyperlane mailbox (delivered(messageId)) plus the recipient's balance.

Fee-free direct route — tested and closed on 2026-11-07: we tested sending directly through the token's warp contract, bypassing the official router, with a real 0.1 USDT send (42 Chain → BSC). It does not work: the warp contracts only accept sends that come from the official BridgeRouter, so every send has to go through that router and its 2 USDT/USDC fee applies. The full test record is in the project README.

Sending FROM BNB Smart Chain and FROM Ethereum works too: their BridgeRouters are older versions (1.1.0 / 1.2.0) whose messaging-fee quote call reverts, so — exactly like the official bridge.zaphod.one frontend — those sends go out with a messaging fee of 0 (deliveries on these routes are proven). No route is disabled: all 26 token routes work. One carries a warning instead — OSAK from 42 Chain to QL1 has zero sends in the contract's whole history, so that direction is unproven (the site says so when you select it; test with a small amount first). Every route was verified live: contract version, paused flag, token address, allowed destination, minimum/maximum and quotes.
